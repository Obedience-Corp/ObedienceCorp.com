The distinction matters. Planning and execution are fundamentally different concerns.

Festival answers: What work needs to happen? In what order? With what success criteria? It's a workspace and methodology — campaigns, festivals, phases, sequences, tasks.

Obey answers: How do agents execute that work? With what providers? Under what constraints? With what proof?

Obey watches Festival's filesystem for state changes. When a task is ready, Obey can spin up an agent session to execute it. When the agent completes, Obey records the result and Festival advances to the next task.

They're designed to work together, but they're independent systems. You can use Festival without Obey (manual execution). You can use Obey without Festival (ad-hoc agent sessions). Together, they're a complete AI development stack.

**Two layers. One stack. Complete control.**
